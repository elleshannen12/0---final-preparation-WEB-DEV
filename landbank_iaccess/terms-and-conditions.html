Online and Digital Banking Services

This agreement explains how your LANDBANK iAccess works, what you're responsible for, and what the bank is and isn't liable for. By checking "I agree" at the bottom of the sign-up flow, you confirm you've read and accept these terms in full.

1

Accessing the service

LANDBANK iAccess is a service the bank makes available at its discretion, for as long as you continue to qualify for it under these terms. It's intended to run 24 hours a day, seven days a week, but the bank doesn't promise uninterrupted access — maintenance windows, technical failures, or events outside its control can make some or all features temporarily unavailable.

  • Entering the wrong password three times in a row locks your account for security reasons.
  • Sessions that sit idle for too long are automatically logged out.
  • Transactions submitted before the daily processing cutoff post the same banking day. Anything submitted after the cutoff, or on a weekend or holiday, is processed on the next banking day.

Banking days run Monday through Friday, excluding holidays.

2

Keeping your account secure

Enrollment

You can enroll an eligible account either in person at a branch, by submitting a signed enrollment form, or entirely online through the platform itself.

Joint accounts

If an account has more than one owner, any transaction made online by one owner is treated as authorized by all owners. Each account holder is expected to have the consent of the others before transacting. The bank may restrict or suspend online access to a joint account at its own discretion.

Paying or transferring to other people's accounts

You can optionally register other accounts — belonging to you at another bank, or to a third party — as transfer or bill-payment destinations. Registering these in advance at a branch means future transfers skip the one-time PIN step; transfers to accounts you haven't pre-registered will always require a one-time PIN sent to your mobile number. Up to five third-party accounts can be registered as destinations at a time.

Transaction alerts

Every financial transaction generates an email or SMS notification to your registered contact details. You're expected to check these regularly to confirm your activity looks correct.

Passwords

Choose a password that's hard for someone else to guess — avoid your name, birthdate, phone number, or simple sequences like "123456." Don't write it down or share it with anyone, including family members, and change it periodically or immediately if you suspect it's been compromised.

One-time PINs (OTP)

Certain transactions require a one-time PIN sent by SMS to your registered mobile number. It's a single-use code meant only for you — never share it with anyone, including someone claiming to be from the bank's support team, except when a bank representative needs it to verify your identity during an official unlock request that you yourself initiated.

3

What you can do online

  • View account activity: real-time balances, holds, and up to 90 days of transaction history for each enrolled account.
  • Loans: apply for a loan, verify and confirm an application, register an approved loan, and view balances, amounts due, and payment history.
  • Bills payment: pay registered or unregistered billers online; paying an unregistered biller requires a one-time PIN.
  • Fund transfers: move money between your own accounts, to registered or unregistered third parties, or to other banks via instant transfer networks. Transfers to your own or pre-registered accounts don't require a one-time PIN; everything else does.
  • Checkbook requests: order a new checkbook online, up to three per account per day. The cost is automatically deducted from your account once the branch approves the order.

The bank's rights and discretion

The bank can restrict, suspend, or shut off your access to some or all features at any time, without needing to give advance notice, if it reasonably believes you've broken these terms, broken the law, or that continued access could put the bank, you, or another client at risk. It also isn't obligated to carry out every transaction you submit — for example, it can decline or reverse a transaction it believes was made in error or resulted from fraud, even after the funds were initially credited.

The bank may also, at its discretion, verify any instruction you send by contacting you directly, and may decline instructions that conflict with its policies even without prior warning.

Your responsibilities and liability

You're responsible for keeping your login ID, password, and one-time PINs confidential, and for every transaction carried out using them — the bank treats any instruction submitted with valid credentials as coming from you and as final. You also agree not to use bots, scrapers, or other automated tools to interact with the platform outside its normal interface.

It's your job to make sure there's enough available balance for any transaction — scheduled, future-dated, or immediate — and any fees or penalties from a failed transaction due to insufficient funds are your responsibility. You're also expected to keep your email address, phone number, and other contact details up to date, since the bank isn't liable for messages that don't reach you because your information was outdated.

If you believe your account has been compromised, or notice any activity you don't recognize, contact the bank immediately using the channels in Section 10.

Fees, changes, and notices

Standard fees apply to certain features and may change over time within legal limits. The bank will publish updated fees and terms on its official website or notify you by email; continuing to use the service after a change takes effect counts as accepting it.

Reporting problems and disputes

You should check your transaction history regularly. If something looks wrong — a transaction you didn't authorize, a missing payment, an incorrect entry — you need to report it to the bank within 20 calendar days of the transaction or statement date. Reports made after that window may be harder for the bank to investigate or resolve in your favor.

8

When the bank isn't liable

Except where a loss is directly caused by the bank's own negligence or misconduct, the bank isn't responsible for losses arising from things outside its reasonable control, including:

  • Natural disasters, power outages, or other force-majeure events;
  • Technical failures in hardware, software, or telecommunications infrastructure, including issues caused by third-party internet or mobile carriers;
  • Unauthorized account access that results from you sharing your credentials, losing your device, or falling for a scam — rather than from a failure on the bank's part.

The bank also doesn't guarantee that a one-time PIN or notification will arrive promptly, since delivery depends in part on your mobile carrier and internet provider.

Ending this agreement

Either you or the bank can end this agreement by giving 10 banking days' written notice. If you request account deletion, it typically takes effect within 5 business days. The bank may also limit or end your access without that notice period if your account becomes dormant, is closed, is subject to a legal hold, or is linked to suspected fraud.
